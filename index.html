Corrections

Our first edition reported that "three of four" Pacifica School District board seats are on the November ballot. The county's qualified-candidate list shows four seats in two contests — three full-term (vote for three) and one short-term (vote for one).

We also called the PSD parcel-tax renewal "Measure D." That was the 2016 measure. This one has no letter yet.

The Lead

Exeunt, Two Councilmembers

Filing closed at 5 p.m. Friday. Mary Bier and Sue Beckmeyer are not on the ballot, District 3 has exactly one candidate, and the city's official notice of who's running is still marked "not yet available."

Three of Pacifica's five council seats are on the November 3 ballot, and only one of them will be defended by the person currently holding it. The other two incumbents — Mary Bier in District 3 and Sue Beckmeyer in District 5 — appear on neither the county's Qualified Candidate List (printed 8/6/2026 4:44:06 PM) nor the City Clerk's own log of nomination papers, which as of this morning carries the header "Last Updated: 8/7/2026 (FINAL)."

Here is the field the two documents agree on:

Dist. 2Christine Boles, "Architect / Businesswoman / Councilmember" — the incumbent Mayor — against Dan Urban, "Retired Regional Director." The only race with an incumbent in it.
Dist. 3Cindy Abbott, "Nonprofit Director." Alone. David Goodwin took out papers on August 5 and did not file.
Dist. 5Margo Meiman, "Retired Teacher," and Sam Casillas, "Climate Resiliency Manager," whose county status still reads "Pending." Justin Olague took papers July 29 and did not file.

The clerk's log contains one line that explains more than it means to. Under California Elections Code §10225, when an eligible incumbent declines to file, the window for that seat stays open five extra days. The log prints that clause exactly once: "Extended Filing Deadline for District 2 (if no filing from eligible incumbent): 5:00pm – Wed., August 12, 2026 (Extended Filing Deadline Not Applicable)." District 2 got the clause. Districts 3 and 5 did not — which reads as a clerk recording that Bier and Beckmeyer were not eligible to run again in the first place. Pacifica voters imposed council term limits by initiative years ago; we could not verify the governing text against a primary document before press time, so we report the paperwork and not the reason. (See the colophon.)

Either way, the arithmetic holds. Barring a write-in campaign — that window opens September 7 — three-fifths of the council seated in December will be new. They will inherit a pier with a ~$64 million repair estimate, a quarry decision the developer's lawyer has promised to make into "a big mess," and, if voters agree, a brand-new penny of sales tax with no expiration date. Two of the people who voted Wednesday to ask for that penny had, by Friday, declined to stand for the job of spending it.

What's genuinely strange is the silence. We found no announcement from either departing councilmember and no coverage of the filing anywhere in the county's press. The city's election page still lists "Notice of Nominees for Public Office" as (not yet available). The most consequential fact about Pacifica's next four years surfaced this week only as an absence in a PDF — two names that aren't there.

City Hall

Five Ayes and a Blank Line

This morning we couldn't confirm the vote. Here it is, read off the signed resolution — because the minutes still don't exist.

The 1-cent sales tax passed unanimously. Resolution No. 61-2026, signed by Mayor Boles and attested by City Clerk Sarah Coffey: "AYES, Councilmembers: BECKMEYER, BIER, BOLES, ESPINOSA, WRIGHT. NOES: N/A. ABSENT: N/A. ABSTAIN: N/A." Five for five, one better than the four-fifths required, after three hours and fifty-seven minutes. We had to read the tally off a scanned signature page because Pacifica has not posted approved council minutes since April 27.

The question voters get, verbatim: the "Pacifica City Services Measure," a 1% (1¢) transactions and use tax "generating approximately $5,000,000 annually until ended by voters," for "general government use." Read those two phrases together: no sunset, and no strings — a general tax, which needs only a simple majority, and which any future council may spend on anything. The measure has no letter yet. The city's own page renders it "Ballot Language of Measure ___ ."

The city's August 6 press release supplies the number that makes the case. City Manager Sean Charpentier: "Today, Pacifica has an $84 million backlog in needed street and road repairs. Without locally controlled funding, those costs are projected to grow to $123 million over the next five years." North County Acting Fire Chief Todd Johnson adds that station houses lack space for crews "to properly decontaminate and store their protective equipment, increasing their potential exposure to carcinogens."

Both true, both grim, and neither is the thing at the end of Beach Boulevard. The release pitches streets, police, fire, 911, parks, beaches, and flooding. Across its full length it does not mention the pier once. The most expensive broken object the city owns is missing from the argument for the city's new money — which is either message discipline or a preview of the answer.

One scheduling note that is its own small comment: written arguments for and against the measure are due to the City Clerk by 5 p.m. Monday, August 10, and rebuttals by 5 p.m. Monday, August 17. Both are Mondays on which Pacifica's government does not meet.

Schools

Nine for Four, and Three for Three

The district that is broke has a real election. The district that is merely poor does not. Pacifica School District — operating under a state "lack of going concern" determination since March, down roughly 17% in enrollment over a decade, one elementary school closed — drew nine candidates for four seats: six for three full-term slots (Bridget Hardt, listed as "Appointed Incumbent"; Rachel Durkin; Grace Sobieski; Tiffany Button; Christopher Swiedler; Katy Bradley) and three for a single short-term seat (Kelly Mendoza; Tracey Simmons; and sitting board clerk Elizabeth Bredall). Nine people volunteering to govern a district whose books alarm its county overseers is the most encouraging fact in this edition.

Jefferson Union High School District — the county's lowest-funded high school district, home of Oceana and Terra Nova, and landlord to a just-approved 1,235-home development on its own land in Daly City — drew exactly three candidates for three full-term seats and one for the short-term seat. Every one of them wins by default. None filed a candidate statement. A district with that much money and land moving through it will seat its board this November without anyone having to make a case.

Worth adding to the tab: JUHSD has a parcel tax measure on the same ballot, alongside PSD's parcel-tax renewal, the city's new penny, four county charter amendments, and the regional Connect Bay Area transit tax. Six money questions, and not one of them has a letter assigned yet.

Pier Watch

Closed since June 4, 2026 — 64 days

No news, and now a measurable silence: the city's 2026 press release archive shows the last pier communication was June 12 — "Emergency Stabilization Activities for the Pacifica Municipal Pier." That is 56 days ago. Since then the city has issued exactly two press releases: one closing the Linda Mar lots overnight, and one asking for a sales tax that doesn't mention the pier. The repair estimate remains ~$64 million against a citywide unfunded capital backlog of roughly $200 million. Day 64.

Briefs

Government goes dark. Monday's City Council meeting is cancelled, as is the August 17 Planning Commission. The next time Pacifica's elected officials convene in public is August 24 — nineteen days after they voted to tax you, and two weeks into a ballot-argument process they will not be in the room for.

Fifty-two people. The tape of Wednesday's 3-hour-57-minute special meeting — the one that put a permanent 1% sales tax on the ballot — has 52 views. Roughly thirteen viewers per hour of deliberation, in a city of about 37,000.

The minutes drought. The city's meeting portal has posted no approved City Council minutes since April 27. Six meetings — including the pier emergency, the budget adoption, and Wednesday's tax vote — exist in the official record only as agendas and video.

Quarry, cont. The Tribune wrote up Monday's denial today, reporting that the 2025 Local Coastal Land Use Plan was certified one day after Preserve@Pacifica filed its preliminary application — the whole dispute turning on a 24-hour gap. "The developer declined to comment on the decision." No litigation had been filed as of press time. (The Tribune's account differs from our record on three dates and figures; see the colophon.)

Neighbors, briefly. Pacifica's National Night Out "culminated at the home of Bryan and Lyla Reinero in Pedro Point" on August 4, per Coastside News — the fourth year the Reineros have hosted, with Police Chief Maria Sarasua, City Manager Charpentier, Mayor Boles and Vice Mayor Wright in the yard. A photo caption records the civic business transacted: "Neighbors bonded and reminisced over drinks and appetizers." Two days later, two of their colleagues let the filing deadline pass.

What we couldn't confirm

Why Bier and Beckmeyer aren't running. We found no statement from either. The clerk's log applies the §10225 extension clause only to District 2, which implies they were term-limited out, but we could not verify Pacifica's term-limit ordinance against a primary text (Ballotpedia and the municipal code both blocked us). We report the paperwork, not the motive.

The last hour. The county's roster prints as of 4:44 p.m. Thursday and the city's log was marked FINAL Friday morning — both before the 5 p.m. Friday close. A walk-in filing in the final hours would not appear in either. Sam Casillas's county status is still "Pending," not "Qualified."

The closed session. Wednesday's agenda carried one unnamed case of anticipated litigation. Whether anything was reported out is unknown; there are no minutes.

Three quarry discrepancies. The Tribune dates the governing quarry ordinance to 1983 (our record, from the agenda packet: Ordinance 391-CS, 1989), the prior ballot defeat to 2016 at nearly 70% (our record: Measure W, 2015, roughly 60–40), and the site at 87 acres (the agenda says 86.39). We have not resolved these; the primary document holds until we do.

Also open. The dollar amount and term of the PSD parcel-tax renewal (the county's resolution PDF won't yield its text). Whether PSD board president Laverne Villalobos is seeking re-election — she is absent from the roster, which may have triggered an extension to August 12. Ballot letters for all six measures. And the "Save Pacifica Pier" Facebook group, formed in June, remains login-walled to us; if the pier story is moving anywhere this week, it is moving there.
